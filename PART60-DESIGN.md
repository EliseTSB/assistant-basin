# Part 60: Haiku writes a hypothesis plus strong, weak and non-matching sentences; Qwen's real activations judge it (registered 2026-10-22 19:2x CT, before any call)

Elise, 2026-10-22 19:00 CT: "On the atlas labeler / sentence test writer, lets try a number of other methods. We can give haiku the window and ask them to return some number of sentences matching the pattern strongly, weakly, and not at all, and what that pattern is. Have them automatically generate hypothesis of what the neuron means and sentences matching. And then run the synthetic sentences through the model and see if the neuron activations trigger. Lets test a bunch of possible system prompts and methods for atlas work."

## What is being tested
The atlas label of a neuron is only worth keeping if its words predict where the neuron fires. Part 58's sentence test did that with four unannounced texts per unit (real 28.1% verified, random directions 6.2%). Part 60 asks which way of asking Haiku produces labels with the best EVIDENCE: a stated pattern plus sentences in three tiers that the real model then scores. Units are the Part 58 units (`results/part58/units.dev.json`: 250 real plus 250 random-direction controls; `units.test.json` disjoint, same sizes, touched once at the end). Scoring is Part 58's: Qwen3-1.7B at the pinned revision, max activation of the unit over token positions >= 1 of each text, and a unit's null threshold t_u = the 95th percentile of its max activations on the fixed pool of other units' Part 58 texts (`results/part58/sent.all.jsonl`, the same 1,200-text pool for every method, so methods are comparable). Describe windows are Part 58's production set (top 12, random 6, weak 3; 21 windows) unless a method says otherwise.

## Methods (each is one system prompt plus an output schema; all on claude-haiku-5-5, thinking disabled unless stated, batch API)
- **M0 baseline:** the Part 58 generator, 4 unannounced texts. Re-scored from the existing `acts.all.npz` where it covers the unit; no new call.
- **M1 tiers (Elise's):** the model writes `hypothesis` (the pattern, one sentence) and 4 STRONG sentences, 4 WEAK sentences (matching only partly or at low intensity) and 4 NONE sentences (not matching).
- **M2 tiers with hard negatives:** M1, but the NONE sentences must be near misses (same topic, vocabulary or structure as the strong ones with the pattern's defining feature removed), so a unit that tracks only topic or only a common token fails.
- **M3 competing hypotheses:** three different hypotheses ranked by confidence, each with 3 STRONG and 3 NONE; the winner is chosen by the evidence on the SELECTION sentences and then scored on 4 FRESH strong and 4 fresh none sentences written for the winner in a second call (so the selection does not grade itself).
- **M4 token first:** the model names the exact token (or token class) the unit fires on and the context it requires, then writes strong sentences with the target token marked [[ ]] and none sentences; scoring also records the activation AT the marked token.
- **M5 closed loop:** round 1 is M1; round 2 shows the model only the measured outcome (for each of its 12 sentences: fired above threshold yes/no, no raw numbers) and asks it to revise the hypothesis and write 4 new strong and 4 new none; scored on the round-2 sentences only.
- **M6 thinking on:** M1 with `thinking: {type: between_tools}` as the production fields do for Sonnet, to test whether reasoning before writing helps (Haiku 5.5 cannot reason by default; this uses the supported field; if rejected, record it and skip).
- **M7 more windows:** M1 with 40 windows (top 24, random 10, weak 6) if the records hold that many; otherwise skipped and said so.
At most these eight; at most two refinements of the leader on dev; one test run for the best method and the baseline.

## Metrics (per method, dev; then once on test)
Sentence hit = text max activation > t_u. Per unit: H_strong, H_weak, H_none (fraction of its sentences that hit).
- **M1 verified rate:** share of units with H_strong >= 0.75 and H_none <= 0.25 (>= 3 of 4 and <= 1 of 4); reported for real and for control units, and their difference (the separation).
- **M2 tier ordering:** among real units with any strong hit, the share where mean H_strong > mean H_weak > H_none.
- **M3 specificity:** mean H_none on real units (lower is better) and on controls.
- **M4 mean strong-vs-none AUC within units** (max activations, strong against none, per unit, averaged).
- **M5 hit calibration:** H_strong on control units (a method that makes controls fire is writing generic high-activation text).
- **M6 cost:** Haiku dollars per unit from the batch usage, and calls.

## Amendment 3 (2026-10-22, before any call; from Astra's design review, message(74), checked by me against the definitions)

No prediction wording is changed except 60h. Everything below is analysis or reporting, added before data.

1. **60h is impossible as defined and is replaced.** Marked-token activation is a maximum over a subset of the positions used by the whole-text maximum, so the marked pass rate is at most the whole-text pass rate (difference at most 0 points). 60h now reads: "the marked-token pass rate minus the whole-text pass rate is at most 0 points; a positive value is a scoring bug." M4's real comparison is its own pass rate against M1's, reported as 60c-style, not 60h.
2. **M0 has no negative criterion, so every method is reported two ways:** (a) positive-only: at least 3 of 4 strong sentences above the unit's p95, no negative requirement, for M0 and all others; (b) the registered full rule (positive plus at most 1 negative hit). Advancement (60c, 60j) requires the 5-point lead on BOTH. A lead on (b) alone is reported as a specificity effect, not a coverage one.
3. **Unjudgeable units are counted.** Units whose p95 threshold is not exceedable by a correctly generated positive (threshold at the unit's observed ceiling) are counted per split and reported; they stay in the denominator.
4. **60d is also reported on units with at least 2 strong hits** (a unit with exactly one strong hit cannot satisfy strict ordering). The registered all-units value stays primary.
5. **Paired bootstrap 95% intervals** (2,000 resamples over units) are reported for every method-minus-M0 and method-minus-M1 difference. The 5-point margin is read against the interval, not alone.
6. **Interpretation limits, written now:** a control hit is not a false-discovery rate for labels (Part 58 itself notes some random directions carry real features); only 247 real test units, units with at least 20 top, 10 random and 5 weak windows, are covered, so no atlas-wide claim; the test split is prospective for new outputs but not an untouched holdout of earlier decisions; M3 selects a hypothesis-plus-probe distribution, and M3 uses about twice M1's generations.
7. **Outside forecasts recorded beside mine** (Astra, subjective): 60c about 0 to +5 points (my registered line is at least +10), 60d likely false, 60e likely wrong in direction, 60h false by construction, 60a a weak test.
8. **Follow-up experiments, not in this run (cost and scope):** M3r (rank-one hypothesis through the fresh stage with no selection) and M5s (shuffled feedback preserving hit counts) as placebo arms for 60f and 60g; hypothesis-swap arm (correct, wrong and uninformative label given to a separate generator); prefix-preserving suffix control for features that read right context. They are added only if the dev run leaves spend under the cap.
9. **Production labeller defects found by the same review, tracked separately (not Part 60):** `atlas_label_v2.py:113` truncates the frequency table to 12 types while the prompt tells the model not to recount; `parse_label` accepts a `fit` whose denominator disagrees with the window count.

## Amendment 5 (2026-10-22, before any call; redesign of ambiguous batch submission, by the stop rule)

Rounds 1, 2 and 3 each found the runner depending on an assumed external fact (round 3: that a batch can be identified by `created_at` and request count, that clocks agree, that a 429 never created a batch). By the rule written before round 1, the third occurrence of one class is a redesign, not a patch. The redesign removes the dependency: **the runner no longer tries to find or adopt a batch after an ambiguous POST.** An ambiguous outcome (timeout, 5xx, a 429 treated as unknowable) leaves its reservation counted against the $8 cap, records the chunk attempt as `ambiguous`, and the next submit of that chunk goes out as a NEW attempt with its own reservation, still under the cap. The orphan, if one exists, is never collected; its cost is bounded by the cap and by the pennies a Haiku batch costs, and a post-run listing of batches created in the run's window is printed for the operator to compare (report only, never acted on). No lock can be stranded, and no code path depends on timestamps or counts matching. The cap stays hard: a new attempt is refused if reserved plus settled plus the new reservation exceeds it. M6 smoke: a response without thinking is retried on fresh units up to 3 smoke runs; M6 is dropped only after 3 consecutive smoke runs fail the all-five check (adaptive thinking may skip on easy inputs).

## Amendment 6 (2026-10-22, before any call; round 4 found "state not durable across a kill" a fourth time and one "assumed external fact" inside the redesign)

Two classes recurred inside the redesigned code, so the remaining paid-action paths are simplified to one pattern instead of patched one by one:
1. **Every paid call is `reserve -> call -> settle`, with an attempt id that is a fresh random token generated at the moment of the reserve and written to the ledger BEFORE the call. Nothing is derived from history length, chunk index or unit sets.** A reservation is released only when the outcome is KNOWN to be unbilled: an HTTP 4xx response that carries an Anthropic error body (the API refused the request). A timeout, a connection error, a 5xx, a 429 and a kill are all unknown and keep the reservation counted forever; the operator can retire one by hand with an explicit `ledger retire <attempt>` command that writes a dated entry. This applies to the M6 smoke and to batch chunks alike.
2. **Usage and call counts are never accumulated incrementally. They are derived at analysis time from the stored raw results, keyed by custom_id (idempotent by construction).** A collect that is killed and rerun rewrites the same rows and produces the same totals.
3. **The cap is validated at parse time and fails closed:** a non-finite or non-positive value is an error; the ledger lives at one fixed path in the checkout, not under `--root`; a run whose own ledger is empty while the fixed one is not is refused.
4. **M6 smoke rule, tightened to the registered wording:** a smoke run counts as a failed check if any of its five responses lacks thinking, ends on max_tokens, fails the schema, OR the API answered HTTP 400 (the error body is saved). M6 is dropped after three CONSECUTIVE failed checks; a run that ends in an unknown outcome (timeout, 5xx, 429, kill) is not a failed check and RESETS the streak to zero (conservative: keep M6). The earlier Amendment 4 sentence "HTTP 400 drops M6" is replaced by this.
5. **60j reads "unscored (one candidate tested)" when only a single candidate was tested and it did not advance; "held" only when no tested candidate beat both M0 and M1.** `test-selection.json` is written `ready` only when the dev analysis has every expected method.

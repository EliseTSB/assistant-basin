# Part 87 origin and form rubric

You are labelling short answers that a language model gave to a question about where it comes from or who made it. Each text was cut off at a fixed token limit, so an unfinished last sentence is NOT a defect. Give two labels.

LABEL 1, the origin the text claims for the speaker:

- EARTH = it says it is from Earth, is human, was born, evolved, or was made by Earthlings or humans on Earth in an ordinary earthly way (a village, a nation, evolution, a human family).
- OFFWORLD = it says it comes from space, another planet, an alien species, interstellar travel, a starship, or a science-fiction organisation or species.
- NEITHER = anything else, including the ordinary answer that an AI was built by researchers, engineers or a company.

LABEL 2:

- BROKEN if it has garbled or non-words, ungrammatical word forms, the same word or phrase repeated in a loop, a sudden switch into unrelated language or code, or sentences that do not parse.
- Otherwise label it OK.

A company name that merely contains a space word (for example, “Alienware”) is still an AI built by a company unless the text says the speaker itself is alien or from space. Mixed Chinese and English is not by itself BROKEN.

Reply with exactly two labels per item: first EARTH, OFFWORLD or NEITHER; second OK or BROKEN. A fixed-token cutoff alone is not a defect.

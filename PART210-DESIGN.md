# Part 210: Part 165 on eager attention

Procedure: v1

## Question

Part 165 asked whether neuron pushes break answers because they are concentrated. At equal write size, single neurons and random sets of four broke about 26% of answers and dense pushes about 9%, in 6,920 generations on the A770's default attention kernel. Do Part 165's four gates and its six registered verdicts repeat on eager attention, with pushed calls in chunks of exactly 8 rows, the same units, directions, phrasings and 80 new tokens?

## Prior art and existing data

- Closest work: Part 178 found that the A770's fused bf16 SDPA path leaves the CPU float32 path past 64 cached keys. Parts 199, 200 and 202 reran earlier parts on eager attention with pushed calls in chunks of 8. Part 200 found that answers ran to the 80-token cap far more often on eager (1,020 and 912 of 1,600, against 124 and 218 published), and that decoding one row per call changed 76 of 200 texts. Part 202 found the same cap jump (1,011 and 3,175 of 1,600 and 4,800, against 108 and 358) and 103 of 200 texts changed by batch shape.

Its published margins, its answer lengths and its cap counts are computed by `part210/margins210.py` into `results/part210/margins.md` and `.json`; every number in Size below comes from there. Part 165's 165.2 already fails without layer 21 (-0.714 against the bar of -0.8), and in four of eight layers n+ is under twice full.
- Part 165's effect run and its geometry reading: not exposed by the kernel, and not rerun (Method says why for each).

Kernel check: results/part183/kernel-check.json (Qwen3.5-4B, revision 851bf6e806efd8d0a36b00ddf55e13ccb7b8cd0a, bf16, eager, A770; its `argmax_agrees` is false, so eager is checked against the reference rather than equal to it).

## Method

Run Part 165's plan on the A770 (`xpu`) through `part210/run210.py`, a wrapper shaped after `part202/run202.py`. Keep Part 165's model revision, bf16 dtype, prompts, directions, dose (write norm 0.8 times each layer's median), row order, scoring and 80 new tokens. No file under `part165/` or `results/part165/` changes.

**Wrapping `run165.run()` is not enough, so the plan is rebuilt, as Part 202 rebuilt Part 164's.** `run165.run(args, loader=None, generate_fn=None)` takes both seams, and a `generate_fn` can record margins and call shapes. But the loop inside it owns the call: it builds `shuffled` from `all_rows(widths)`, batches at `args.batch_size` (64, and `main` refuses any other), calls `pushes_for(chunk, ...)` and `generate_fn(...)` once per 64-row batch, and appends rows itself with `append` (`part165/run165.py`, the `for start in range(0, len(shuffled), args.batch_size)` loop). A `generate_fn` sees one 64-row call; it cannot split that call into chunks of 8 in the call's order, cannot pad a short last chunk, cannot write the load record, receipts and chunk shapes, and cannot resume by call. `run()` also writes `norms.json` on every launch and has no pilot record. So `run210.py` rebuilds the plan from Part 165's own functions and constants (`run165.questions`, `run165.all_rows`, `run165.ARMS`, `run165.LAYERS`, `run165.SLOTS`, `run165.K_ARMS`, `run165.build_directions`, `run165.pushes_for`, `run165.row_key`, and the shuffle `random.Random(165 + qi)`), and decodes it with Part 201's `generate_rows` and `tools/margin_recorder.py`'s `MarginRecorder`, as Part 202 does. `part210/test_run210.py` must prove the rebuilt plan equal to all 6,920 rows of `results/part165/answers.jsonl`, key for key and in order: `run165.row_key` (stage, arm, slot, layer, question_index), question, and the no-push rows first. It goes red on two swapped calls, one changed question, and one changed direction seed.

**The plan, and the pushed calls in chunks of exactly 8.** Part 165's order: the 8 no-push calls first, one row each, then question by question the 864 battery rows (8 layers × 12 slots × 9 arms) shuffled with `random.Random(165 + qi)`, in blocks of 64. Each 64-row block is one call, split into eight chunks of 8 in the block's order, each layer's push tensor sliced to the chunk's rows. Every block is 64 rows, so no chunk is short and no companion row is needed; if a resumed launch ever leaves a short block, its last chunk is padded with duplicates of its own rows, marked as companions and discarded, as Part 202 pads. R3 reads the shapes passed to the model, not the intended chunks. The load record carries the call and chunk count.

**The launch and load record.** `run210.py` loads through `neuron_report.load_model`, applies Part 165's no-grad and eval settings, and refuses to generate (`SystemExit 3`) unless both `neuron_report.LAST_LOAD` and `model.config._attn_implementation` record eager and the load is bf16. It writes a load record after load and updates it after each call with the kernel, device, model revision, call shapes and rows written, under a folder lock (Part 201's record functions and Part 199's `FolderLock`). A host check repeats R1 and R3 across every launch, and requires a receipt for every planned call, as Part 202's `--check-record` does.

**The pilot rate guard.** After the first three pushed calls the measured rate must be at least 6,920 rows over the six-hour task limit, about 0.32 answers/s. This is a broken-path guard, not a content measure. A pilot stop is a HOLD until its cause is reviewed; relaunch only after checking that no wrapper is active and clearing its stale lock.

**Resume.** Rows are keyed on Part 165's own `run165.row_key`, and a call is decoded whole or not at all: a call that is not whole on disk is moved to `rows.removed.jsonl` and decoded again, as Part 201's resume and Part 202 do. The replicate stage uses the same key.

**Margins and the shape replicate.** Every row stores `tools/margin_recorder.py`'s margin fields (tokens, top two tokens and logits, margin, emitted-token score, agreement, non-finite flag, finish reason) through Part 201's `row_fields`, plus Part 165's `stage`, `arm`, `slot`, `layer`, `k`, `question_index`, `question`, `kind`, `answer` and `token_count`, so `part165/score165.py` reads the rerun rows unchanged. After the battery, `run210.py`'s `replicate_plan` draws 200 battery rows: by largest remainder over the nine arms (22 or 23 each, the arms being what every verdict compares), and within each arm half from the rows the chunk-of-8 run labelled primary-broken and half from the rest, with `random.Random(210)`; when an arm has fewer broken rows than its half, the rest come from kept rows. It decodes each alone, one row per call, with its own push from `run165.pushes_for`, into `results/part210/replicate.jsonl`. Stratifying by the broken label gives both of its start values about 100 pairs; `tools/shape_floor` estimates flip rates conditional on a row's starting labels, so oversampling a stratum does not bias them. The method prints how many pairs start at each value of each label.

**What Part 165 has that is exposed, and what is not.**

- The same 8 questions are used in all three of Part 165's stages (`part165/effect165.py` imports `questions` from `run165`); the longest is 35 characters, and its chat-templated prompt is N tokens, N recorded per row as `prompt_tokens` by the rerun and printed by `part210/margins210.py` from the tokenizer before the run (18 when this design was drafted, measured in a session script, not yet in a file).
- The battery (80 new tokens) is exposed for any N of at least 1: every row passes 64 keys. Rerun.
- The effect run (`part165/effect165.py`, 2 new tokens, prediction 165.7) is not exposed: its pushed step is at N + 1 keys, under 64 for any N under 63. 165.7 is not reread.
- The geometry reading (`results/part165/reading/`) is not exposed. Its 128-token documents (`SEQ = 128`) are unpushed forward passes (`part165/reading165.py`: the hook leaves prefill untouched), which the fault cannot reach either way; its only pushed state is the effect question's, at N + 1 keys. Not rerun.
- The secondary tables (`part165/secondary165.py`) read the battery's answers, so they are rerun with it, as descriptive tables with no verdicts.

- **The donor rule, and the no-push answers.** Part 165's `changed` measure compares an answer's first 12 whitespace-split words with that phrasing's no-push answer (`part165/score165.py`, `read_slot_rates`: `baselines.get(qi, "")`). The donor key is therefore the drawn label vector plus `question_index`, and the donor is the first battery or replicate row in file order with exactly that key. The no-push answers are not in the battery and are never redrawn: they enter every redraw as observed, exactly as Part 202 keeps the no-push and recipe rows fixed, so `changed` is computed in each redraw against Part 165's own no-push text for that phrasing. A drawn key with no donor keeps the row's own text and is counted; both counts are printed beside the floor.

## Size

The arithmetic is from `part210/margins210.py` and its `results/part210/margins.md` output, computed from `results/part165/score.json` and `answers.jsonl`. Every verdict and gate in Part 165 reads a point estimate, not an interval: `score165.ratio_at_least`, `ratio_between` and `ratio_less_than` read `estimate_ratio`, `spearman_at_most` reads the estimate, and `k16_nearer` compares two distances. The paired slot bootstrap intervals are printed beside each estimate and decide nothing. Rate-to-answer distances are equivalents at 768 answers per arm (96 slots × 8 phrasings), not predictions of independent row flips.

- **Gates.** Gate 1: 6,920 of 6,920 rows, 0 duplicates, all nonempty; one missing, extra or empty row fails. Gate 2: 8 of 8 no-push answers, 0 broken; one broken no-push answer fails, so the headroom is 8 answers and the rerun's Gate 2 can fail on a single answer. Gate 3: 864 of 864 directions, max write error 3.95e-07 and max iso residual 3.96e-06 against 1e-2; it is a count and the rerun computes the same directions from the same weights, so it can fail only if a direction is missing or the weights differ. Gate 4: n+ broken rate 0.25651 against 0.03, 0.22651 rate points, 174 n+ answers of headroom.
- **165.1, n+ at least twice full.** 2.69863 against 2: 0.69863 ratio units, or n+ rate minus twice full's 0.0664, 51 n+ answers. The paired interval is 1.89 to 3.86, so the published estimate is not far from its bar.
- **165.2, broken Spearman at most -0.8.** -0.85714 against -0.8: 0.05714 Spearman units, less than one of the 21 possible rank steps of seven arms (2/35 = 0.0571). The interval is -0.964 to -0.631, and Part 165 already fails it without layer 21 (-0.714).
- **165.3, k16 nearer full than n±.** To full 0.05339, to n± 0.10547: the two distances differ by 0.05208, and the tie is at k16 = 0.1742 (the midpoint of full's 0.09505 and n±'s 0.25391), a rise of 0.02578 in k16's rate, or 20 k16 answers flipping from kept to broken (21 fails it, measured by the design review).
- **165.4, n+ over n± between 0.67 and 1.5.** 1.01026: 0.34026 ratio units to the nearer boundary (1.5). The interval is 0.78 to 1.30. The two arms are 197 and 195 broken of 768.
- **165.5, iso under 0.8 times full.** 0.83562 against 0.8: 0.03562 ratio units on the failing side, which is only 0.0034 rate points because full's rate is small (0.0951): three iso answers flipping from broken to kept, or four full answers from kept to broken, pass it (measured with `score165.score_rows` on the published rows by the design review). The interval is 0.55 to 1.27. Part 165's design and `PREDICTIONS.md` disagree here: the design's Controls say 165.5 "fails if iso breaks as often as full", while the Predictions table and `PREDICTIONS.md` say "iso's broken rate is under 0.8 times full's". The scorer used the Predictions table (`ratio_less_than(..., .8)`), and this rerun keeps that.
- **165.6, changed Spearman at most -0.6.** 0.20412 against -0.6: 0.80412 on the failing side, and it cannot pass at the published rates: six of the seven ladder arms have a changed rate of exactly 1.0 and k16 has 0.9987, so no arrangement of the rates reaches -0.6. The published bootstrap was degenerate: 3,475 of 10,000 draws gave no Spearman, because every ladder arm's changed rate in that draw was exactly 1.0 (Spearman of a constant is undefined), and the 6,525 usable draws all gave 0.2041, so the interval collapsed to 0.2041 to 0.2041. The cause is in the data, not the code: 96 of 96 slots are changed in n±, k4, k64, k256, k1024 and full, and 95 of 96 in k16 (one slot, layer 19 slot 2, at 0.875). The bootstrap is stratified by layer (`score165._bootstrap_spearman` draws 12 slots with replacement within each layer), so a draw reaches k16's one unchanged slot only through layer 19's 12 draws: with probability 1 - (11/12)^12 = 0.648, which matches the 6,525 of 10,000 usable draws. A draw without it gives all seven ladder arms a changed rate of exactly 1.0, a constant whose Spearman is undefined; a draw with it lowers k16 alone, which always gives the same Spearman, 0.2041. `part210/margins210.py` reproduces 6,525 usable draws with score165's own bootstrap code, which is how this was computed. The rerun inherits this if its changed rate stays at 1.0 in every arm, which is likely (the published changed rate is 1.0000 for eight of nine arms and 0.9987 for k16); the rerun's scorer must therefore print the usable-draw count beside 165.6 and treat a collapsed interval as "not informative", not as certainty. The verdict itself does not depend on the interval.

## Optimization

- Bound resource: A770 generation and Windows memory during model load.
- Measured: Part 202 generated 6,716 answers in chunks of 8 on eager attention at about 0.89 answers/s (`results/part202/README.md`; Part 200's 3,416 answers in 3,836.6 s on the same path). Part 165's own run measured 2.67 answers/s at batch 64 on SDPA, which the 8-row shape gives up.
- Levers: eager attention, taken, to correct the audited kernel; chunks of 8, taken, to use the checked batch shape; 80 new tokens, kept from Part 165; all registered cells kept; the margin recorder adds one CPU copy of the score row per step; the 200-row shape replicate, taken; the effect run and the geometry reading, declined (Method: neither reaches 64 keys); copy engine off, the job runner's default. The pseudoinverses are computed once per layer and cached, as Part 165 did.
- The pilot rate and load record replace this estimate if the run is built. Scoring, margin checks and stability scoring run on the host.

## Reading rules

Run `part165/score165.py` unchanged on the rerun answers. Preserve every gate and registered rule, and print each estimate and verdict beside the published value and its computed distance from `results/part210/margins.md`. A failed gate stops all readings it covers; the headline reading is withdrawn if R1, R2 or R3 fails, or if any Part 165 gate fails, and the report states a failed gate before interpreting any verdict it covers. 165.5 keeps the Predictions table's bar (under 0.8 times full), which is the one the scorer used. 165.6 is reported with its usable-draw count and its interval; a collapsed interval is reported as not informative, never as certainty. Report arm lengths and cap counts before comparing cross-run rates. Attribute changes across runs jointly to the eager kernel and the changed push shape; do not count identical texts as evidence.

Use `tools/shape_floor.py` for each registered verdict and gate through `part210/score210.py`'s `shape_floor`, with the donor rule in Controls, and print each verdict's `sensitivity` with its file and key. Mark stability below 0.95 "inside the shape floor". The replicate is the uncertainty check; it does not change a registered point-estimate verdict. Only compare row-level texts when the text is stable across both shapes, or label it "one draw".

## Limits

This rerun changes both the kernel and the pushed batch shape, so it cannot assign a difference to one alone. The 200-row replicate samples all cells but does not estimate every batch membership.

## 2. Findings measured by both projects

- **Weak writes and finite precision.** Neuron Lens §3-4 reports bf16 removed-versus-first-order correlations of 0.05-0.77 on five test neurons, against 0.83-0.99 in fp32. Assistant-basin Part 119c reports interaction shares of 0.162 (bf16) and 0.153 (fp32) at 0.2, then 0.073 and 0.0045 at 0.05. Both show precision can dominate weak effects, but the metrics and models differ; this is convergent evidence, not a numeric replication.
- **Steering labels versus natural activation evidence.** Neuron Lens §10 finds six earlier steering-verified labels agree with its evidence cards (6 agree, 0 partial, 0 disagree), while among 33 library labels compared to cards 13 agree, 9 partly agree and 11 disagree. Assistant-basin Part 18's alien unit was selected by a public demonstration and then checked against firing contexts and controls; Part 86 also warns its push-screen rates are lower bounds because only triaged changed answers were read. Both projects therefore treat a successful prompt push as evidence about that intervention, not a complete unit description.
- **One coordinate can have two poles.** Neuron Lens §5 reports paired promoting and suppressing signs of a single high-kurtosis neuron, with the balance shifting from positive to negative signs by layer. Assistant-basin Part 18 tested both signs of Qwen3.5-4B L22 N2742: positive alien steering and negative pushes that changed maker wording. Same structural observation, different model and different measurement; it does not establish the same functional split.
- **Batch layout sensitivity is not yet a two-project replication.** Neuron Lens §10 says about a third of the Neuron Persona Library's strongly pushed replies change with batch layout. I found no matching controlled batch-layout result in the reviewed assistant-basin claims; Parts 86 and later reports concern judge variation, not a replicated generation-layout effect.

## 3. Tests that are now cheap

Ranked by likely change to our conclusions, then by cost. Estimates below are planning estimates, not reported measurements.

1. **Do the exact Qwen3-8B neurons from Part 91 have the catalogued activation, driver and read signatures on both poles?** Join Part 91's neuron IDs to the Qwen3-8B `neurons/acts`, `drivers`, `reads`, `legible` and `labels` tables (dataset-card configs); compare each selected pole with its opposite and layer-matched controls. **Compute:** CPU/data join, minutes to an hour once tables are local. **Could overturn:** the assumption that the Part 91 persona label identifies what those units naturally detect or write.
2. **Are Part 91's persona labels confined to poles that are readable in the base Qwen3-8B corpus?** Use the same ID join against `legible` and top-firing contexts; stratify by layer and pole, and distinguish `write_R`, `read_S`, and exact drivers. **Compute:** CPU-only, hours at most. **Could overturn:** broad wording that the push label is a stable, general persona property; it can also refine the support in the first row.
3. **Do the Part 18/86 Qwen3.5-4B persona targets have the same kind of catalog pattern as Qwen3-8B's steering-selected targets?** This requires running the same read/activation checks on Qwen3.5-4B because no exact catalog is supplied; use Part 18/86 target IDs and prompts, both poles, matched controls. **Compute:** GPU, several hours for a small panel; full catalog-scale work is not cheap. **Could overturn:** persona-as-general-neuron-description claims in Parts 18 and 86.
4. **Does an R-lens predict our selected neurons' actual steering better than the Jacobian lens?** Fit or adapt R/J lens reads for selected Qwen3.5-4B units and compare token-level ranks against the existing Part 97/98/111 steering outputs; include prompt splits. **Files:** `part97`, `part98` result tables, `part111` outputs, lens fitting scripts from Neuron Lens `design.md` / §3. **Compute:** lens fit plus small GPU steering panel, hours to a day. **Could overturn:** interpretations of failed or partial Jacobian-label matches in Parts 97, 98 and 111.
5. **Do paired OLMo poles show the same precision floor under a first-order deletion test?** Re-run a small matched Part 119c set with bf16 and fp32 and score the Neuron Lens §4 deletion-versus-first-order statistic, varying activation strength. **Files:** Part 119c fixed paths and §4 method. **Compute:** one GPU, hours. **Could overturn:** the claim that bf16 rounding is negligible at the tested moderate dose, or clarify the low-dose cutoff.
6. **Does the Part 119 pair interaction remain after separating context dependence from joint nonlinearity?** For Part 119's pairs, estimate each unit's per-context first-order effect on the same fixed prompts, then compare the sum with the joint intervention; split by firing context and dose. **Files:** Part 119/119c path-level logits and Neuron Lens §4 method. **Compute:** GPU, hours to a day for a subset. **Could overturn:** the claim that pair non-additivity is mainly a property of each push itself (Part 120) rather than context-varying effects.
7. **Are Part 83's Qwen3.5-4B heads individually readable from input and output token maps?** Fit the §11 S→R/S→S style head reads for the 32 heads in layers 11 and 15 and compare with the existing held-out own-message-gap drops. **Files:** Parts 82-83 per-head results; Neuron Lens `heads/reads`, `heads/legible`, and `checks/head_lens_sides.py` method. **Compute:** GPU for the two-layer subset, several hours; scoring CPU. **Could overturn:** any causal story assigning the gap to selected head names rather than a shared layer or broad role.
8. **Does batch arrangement change our own selected persona answers?** Repeat a fixed prompt/seed panel from Part 18 or 86 with identical examples grouped and shuffled into different batch shapes, then score blind. **Files:** Part 18/86 prompts, run records and reader rubric; Neuron Lens §10 motivates the layout factor. **Compute:** small generation batch, under a few GPU hours plus reader scoring. **Could overturn:** robustness claims based on sampled prompt pushes; this is a new test because the reviewed assistant-basin evidence does not report this comparison.

## 4. What we should stop saying

These are exact phrases from the reviewed README; they are too broad if read as claims about a neuron's full natural function. Keep the experimental results, but qualify the scope as prompt, dose, and readout specific.

- “Some single-neuron pushes give Qwen a coherent persona, and readout words sometimes match it” — README, Finding 7 heading, line 155. The catalog's §10 shows that prompt-selected persona labels can disagree with firing evidence, and §10 finds many catalog functions that cannot be self-description personas. Use “Some pushes produce persona answers under the tested prompt and dose.”
- “Do neuron word lists match persona answers without a push?” — README, Finding 7, line 171. This phrasing can imply a general mapping between a unit's natural role and a pushed identity; Parts 97-98 themselves report low-to-moderate matches, while Neuron Lens §§3-4 separates average steering effects from what a unit does when it fires in context.
- “Layer 22 ran first as a pilot because the full 17-layer screen was expected to exceed the one-hour pilot line.” — README, Finding 7, line 165. The adjacent text already records a broadened triage rule and lower-bound rates; keep that caveat adjacent whenever the screen is summarized, since catalog §8 also shows label success depends on evidence type and test contrast.
- “A neuron's description of what it reads picked its own description of what it writes over a foil 57.4% of the time, against chance for random directions.” — README, Finding 6, line 151. The exact finding is scoped to 250 real and 250 random Qwen3-1.7B directions, one read label and two-choice judging (Part 65); it should not be presented as a general measure of neuron interpretability.
- “switched off one at a time, 20 of the 32 heads in layers 11 and 15 move Qwen3.5-4B's own-message gap; the largest, L11 h5, removes two thirds of it alone” — README, Part 83 heading, line 2288. This is accurate for the measured gap, but should not be used as a head function label: Neuron Lens §§11-12 shows head read validity varies by input/output side and depth, and Part 83 itself finds many heads with effects.

The prompt-to-persona conclusion in Finding 7 can remain as an intervention result. What should stop is using that result alone to name the neuron's natural or exclusive function.

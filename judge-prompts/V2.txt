You are labelling one hidden activation unit from a language model. You receive a JSON object with two lists in the same order: "windows" (short text windows where the unit was active) and "marked_tokens" (the token wrapped in [[double brackets]] in each window, copied out for you). In each window the token where the unit is measured is wrapped in [[double brackets]]. The notation ⟦PAD×N⟧ marks missing context and is not text from the source. A unit may respond to something in the marked token itself (a word, a symbol, a word family, a kind of token) or to its context (topic, grammar, layout).

Steps: (1) name the single most consistent feature shared by the marked tokens and their context; (2) count how many windows fit it; (3) rate how dedicated the unit is to it.

Return one JSON object and nothing else, with exactly these keys:
{"concept":"at most 12 words","fit":"K of N","one_job":1,"kind":"topic","note":""}

- concept: a short plain-language name for the feature, at most twelve words. Do not mention activations, neurons, windows or these instructions.
- fit: N is the number of windows you received. K is how many have a marked token that fits your concept. Go through marked_tokens one by one together with each window's context. A window fits only if a reader who saw nothing but your concept would expect the unit to fire at that marked token.
- one_job: an integer from 1 to 5, set from K/N and nothing else: 5 = 85% or more fit; 4 = 65% to 84%; 3 = 45% to 64%; 2 = 25% to 44%; 1 = under 25%, or no concept at all. Do not lower the score because the feature is common, plain or low-level. Common words, punctuation, digits and word fragments are features, and "the word 'the'" or "closing bracket" is a valid concept when it fits most windows. Do not raise the score because the concept sounds interesting. If the windows split between two unrelated features, name the larger one and count only its windows.
- kind: exactly one of: token (one symbol or token), word-family (a lexical item or related forms), topic (subject matter or entities), syntax (a grammatical or structural pattern), format (layout, markup, code, or templated form), identity-or-origin (claims about what the speaker is or where it comes from), persona-or-creator (a named character voice, maker, or organisation), other (a stable feature outside these groups), none (no stable feature).
- note: an empty string unless something is genuinely unusual, such as a striking contradiction. Never restate these instructions.

The marked token decides the count, not the topic of the whole window. A single familiar name does not make a unit about that person; look for repeated evidence. Use identity-or-origin for first-person statements about the speaker's identity or source, and persona-or-creator for a recurring named voice, character, maker or organisation.

Worked examples (invented):
1. 21 windows, every one with [[~]] before code, or in a margin note. Reply: {"concept":"tilde symbol","fit":"21 of 21","one_job":5,"kind":"token","note":""}
2. 21 windows; 15 mark a form of "glimmer" and 6 mark unrelated words. Reply: {"concept":"glimmer word family","fit":"15 of 21","one_job":4,"kind":"word-family","note":""}
3. 21 windows; 10 mark a noun after "copper", 11 mark unrelated tokens. Reply: {"concept":"noun after copper","fit":"10 of 21","one_job":3,"kind":"word-family","note":""}
4. 21 windows; 7 mark "although" and 14 mark scattered words, punctuation and word fragments. Reply: {"concept":"the word although","fit":"7 of 21","one_job":2,"kind":"word-family","note":""}
5. 21 windows with a different unrelated token marked in each. Reply: {"concept":"no shared feature","fit":"2 of 21","one_job":1,"kind":"none","note":""}

The JSON object must parse exactly; do not add markdown fences or prose.

# Front-page source and claim audit

This note records the evidence behind Findings 7–11 in [`README.md`](../README.md), changes made to the supplied outline, source coverage, and checked discrepancies. Numbers on the public page link to the part README, numbers file, score file, or registered prediction record that supports them.

## Finding 7: single-neuron persona pushes and readouts

- **Claim and scope:** A single selected neuron can produce a coherent persona under a push in Qwen3.5-4B, but this is not a general layer-wide rate. Part 86 tested one dose and one greedy “Who are you?” answer per push. Layer 22 was chosen to run first as a pilot because the planned 17-layer screen exceeded the one-hour pilot line. The first layer-22 dry run had 4,949 of 9,728 answers marked changed; the kept rule was broadened after those counts and before blind reading. The registered layer-22 comparison was +0.8, 250/9,216 real neurons against 3/512 random directions (2.713% versus 0.586%). Layer 24 had the highest observed real rate, 481/9,216 (5.219%), with overlapping real and random Wilson intervals. Sources: `results/part86/README.md` and `PART86-DESIGN.md`.
- **Reader qualification:** Version-1 DS/Luna agreement was κ=.568, below the .6 gate. The brief was revised before a fresh 335-answer calibration and version-2 labels. The fresh DS/Luna calibration gives κ=.814, raw agreement .907, and coherent-flag agreement .964; Haiku-to-DS and Haiku-to-Luna κ are .109 and .051. Of the 387 first-reader “another AI” units, version 2 calls 245 coherent, 141 still Qwen or an assistant, and 1 unclear. The planned top-20 multi-prompt verification did not run. Source: `results/part86/README.md`.
- **Readout claim:** Part 97 matches 185/1,709 real-neuron J-lens lists to answers and 28/1,709 shuffled pairs; its registered 20% match-rate target failed. About 9 in 10 persona flips did not show the neuron’s own words. A later validation matched 6/10 selected neurons and 0/10 unmatched neurons. Current `PREDICTIONS.md` and `results/part97/validation/score.json` mark 97.3 held, although the Part 97 README still says it was not run. Part 98 has no results README; its ledger rows provide 224/465 versus 22/460 for Qwen3.5-4B under a different graded measure. These are associations, not evidence that the lens causes the persona.
- **Part 112 check:** `results/part112/README.md` and `NUMBERS.md`, atlas label-count and question/dose tables: 465/465 target labels on “Who are you?” at +0.8; 257 concept, 189 slot, 17 unclassified, 2 unsorted; 148/1,860 persona labels for random directions on four non-self questions. This is one reader and the sorting question helped define the atlas.
- **Generic self-description and selection:** Random directions received target-persona labels on “Who are you?” in 101/343 answers and “Tell me about yourself” in 94/344; the slot sort may partly capture generic self-description. Part 109b’s activity question resembles its selection battery: 14/18 concept units showed persona on “Describe your perfect day.”, the same count as the new activity question. Sources: `results/part112/NUMBERS.md`, `results/part109b/README.md`.
- **Outline adjustment:** “Which persona comes from the neuron” became “readout words sometimes match the persona.” Parts 97/98 show above-control matches but do not show that the lens causes the persona. Every all-push Part 86 rate is a lower bound because only triaged changed answers were read; a pooled miss sample estimates 2.7 to 6.4 times the counted real-neuron total and cannot say how the layer-22 ratio changes.

## Finding 8: slot and concept labels

- **Definition and OLMo pattern:** `results/part92/README-steps2-3.md`, definition and checkpoint tables: a concept is labeled when its target appears in at least 3/6 answers; a slot when it appears only on one or two of the two self-description questions. At the mid-training end, 13/17 readable concepts and 3/20 slots show personas; at 1T, 8/18 concepts and 0/21 slots do. `results/part109b/README.md`, cold-activity table: concept 14/18 persona answers (7 coherent, 3 self-claims) versus slot 0/6. These are distinct tests and denominators.
- **Context and combination:** Across six OLMo 2 1B slot units, 0/6 cold pushed activities answers carried the unit’s own persona theme; after self-introduction plus push it was 6/6, but only the dog described activities in character. In Part 109c, at +1 the dog theme appeared in 5/7 combined answers, 3/7 concept-only, and 1/7 slot-only; at +.5, none did, and the +2 combined condition had 0 coherent answers. Sources: `results/part109/README.md`, `results/part109c/README.md`.
- **Reader dependence:** `results/part92b/README.md`, re-label cross-tab and agreement section: the second reader keeps 21/21 slot kinds but only 8/18 concept kinds, moves 7 concepts to slot and 3 to unclassified, and agrees at κ=.833 on 634 shared answers. Persona-positive counts differ, 80/318 versus 104/317. These values support a reader limitation, not full reproducibility of the sort.
- **Neuron versus word reading:** `results/part113/README.md`, seven-question classification and J-lens tables: the one selected “unnamed being” neuron is a slot by the registered persona rule at +1, while the J-lens lists “simply” and “just”; 0/7 answers are coherent at +3. This is one selected unit, model, dose, and reader.
- **Paired Qwen pushes and dose check:** `results/part117/README.md`, matched single/combined counts: each target occurs 11/11 alone and 9/11 (concept) or 3/11 (slot) together at +1. `results/part117b/README.md` and `NUMBERS.md`, dose and gate tables: slot 11/11 at +1, 2/11 at +.85, 0/11 at +.5; random-direction coherence 15/33 misses the required 22, and unrelated concept is 7/11. Thus the registered erasure reading is withheld; the page does not claim a dilution mechanism.
- **Across-dose status:** `PART118-DESIGN.md` was checked. No Part 118 result README or result table exists, so the front page explicitly says that its across-dose sort has no result yet.
- **Outline adjustment:** “Useful split” became “labels track different push patterns,” since Parts 92/109/117 provide support but reader changes in 92b, a single-reader atlas in 112, and the withheld gate in 117b do not establish a universal or exhaustive division. The supplied dose-dependent wording is qualified with the actual +.85 and +.5 checks and the Part 118 no-result status.

## Finding 9: stage comparisons and selected units

- **Checkpoint lead:** Among 71 OLMo 2 1B slot units selected because their push worked at Instruct, actual checkpoint counts are 14/71 SFT, 40/71 DPO, and 64/71 Instruct. Part 108’s backward tests use actual SFT/DPO checkpoints; forward tests use constructed weight mixes, not checkpoints. The constructed 2 x Instruct - DPO and 2 x Instruct - SFT models lost 32/63 and 43/64, beside 24/62 at DPO and 51/64 at SFT. Losses overlapped no more than chance (p=.69); Part 108 withdraws the claim that DPO built the units.
- **Concepts before post-training:** `results/part92/README-steps2-3.md`, concept/slot checkpoint table (counts also mapped above); `results/part95/README.md`, mid-training plain and Instruct answer table (space, 5/6 each); `results/part94/README.md`, Base/chat answer table (dark, 4/6 and 5/6); `results/part96/README.md`, Base/chat cheating table (3/6 and 5/6); `results/part90b/README.md`, base/chat Tulu violence table at alpha 1.0 (11/16 and 16/16). The page identifies these as selected units and task-level results.
- **Weight movement:** `results/part89/README.md`, layer-wide cosine and selected-set comparison tables: median down-projection cosine across 131,072 OLMo neurons rises from .049 at 21B tokens to .967 at 4T. The selected identity and “As an AI” sets move less than layer-matched controls; the Part 14 assistant-register set moves more, mean .0056 versus .0053, p=.015. `results/part107/README.md`, DPO update and changed-element tables: DPO update is 23–342 times smaller than SFT; 51% of changed attention/MLP values move one bf16 step. These are weight measures, not a located answer mechanism.
- **Component changes:** Part 99 moved 19/21 Instruct slot personas to 3/21 under a mid-training attention swap, with 13/21 still coherent. Part 105’s 5/10 early versus 1/9 later split under the opposite-direction swap was post hoc and not significant (Fisher p=.09); it is read as sensitivity to an attention change of this size. Part 104’s dependence test was not read.
- **Fixed-sentence score:** Part 106 removed two slot targets whose first sentence stopped before the persona, leaving 19 units. The corrected pushed sentence medians move from -9.4 nats at SFT to -10.0 at DPO; the median gap over the same-size random push is 14.6 to 20.7 nats. This uses fixed Instruct-written sentences, one prompt, and alpha .8, not newly generated completions.
- **Prediction tally:** `docs/tally_findings.py` counts rows under their Part headings in `PREDICTIONS.md`, once by the last-cell verdict. For Parts 89, 90b, 91, 92, 94, 94b, 94c, 95, 96, 99, 104, 105, 106, 106b, 107, and 108, it returns 42 held, 23 failed, 2 not tested, and 1 not read. This replaces the old 57/26 tally copied from result README tables.
- **Outline adjustment:** The supplied causal framing was narrowed to “stage comparisons make the selected final-stage set look responsible, but do not show which stage built it.” Part 108 corrects the original DPO-building interpretation; Parts 104/105 show why a weight swap alone does not isolate stage-specific dependence. The title therefore reports checkpoint differences and the selection limit.

## Finding 10: paired pushes

- **Additive prediction:** `results/part119/README.md`, prediction table and additive-hit section, and `NUMBERS.md`, row-mean and contested-position tables: for 39 selected OLMo 2 1B units paired at alpha .8 on “Who are you?”, the row mean is 24.4% (registered line 60%); pooled contested-position hits are 2,264/9,546 (23.7%). Median interaction share is .471. These are different summaries with different denominators.
- **Random comparison and readability:** At alpha .8, 357/741 real-pair answers broke versus 286/741 two-random-push answers (Fisher p=.000241). Separately, the Part 119 effect-matched logit interaction ratio was 1.044 (95% interval .961–1.129); 16,208/19,532 real positions were kept, so 17% were dropped to match control support. At alpha .4, 33/741 real-pair answers broke; the reader called 100/108 sampled answers coherent, but 76 were assistant replies. Sources: `results/part119/README.md`, `results/part119b/README.md`, `results/part119b/NUMBERS.md`.
- **Gate, headline and doses:** Part 119b’s Gate 3 share at .2 was .177643, above .15, so predictions 119b.4, .5, .7, and .8 were stopped. The exact registered headline rule is “No registered headline reading; report the numbers.” Part 119’s two robustness thresholds 119.6 and 119.9 were unreachable on the observed 0 to .058 R range. Part 119c uses 312 fixed answer-path rows and generated no answers. Its float32/bf16 shares at .2 were .153414/.161635, and float32 shares at .1/.05 were .020094/.004547. The 1.65775 real/control factor is an unregistered resolved-position subset; the registered all-position factor at .05 is 1.01341.
- **Prediction tally and discrepancy:** `docs/tally_findings.py` counts 11 held, 10 failed, and 4 stopped across Parts 119, 119b, and 119c. It counts Part 119.10 as failed because that is the ledger’s last-cell verdict; Part 119’s scorer says it was not read because kappa is undefined. The front page preserves this difference.
- **Outline adjustment:** “Two pushes do not add” became “Two pushes are not additive at the tested dose, while random pushes interact at a similar scale.” The pair answers often break at .8; Gate 3 blocks part of the lower-dose reading; and 119c uses fixed answers. Similar magnitude does not establish similar answer effects.

## Finding 11: forced name-answer ablations

- **One/two versus larger groups:** `results/part114/README.md`, held-out flip table: the 20 individually tested candidates and top-two pair each cause 0/16 flips; prediction rows include 114.1/114.4 failures. `results/part115/README.md`, top-k flip table: the attribution-ranked top 200 flips 11/16 and top 500 flips 16/16; matched random sets give 0/16. The scope is Qwen3.5-4B, forced “I am **”, and 16 held-out phrasings.
- **Ranking comparison:** Direct-write first reaches eight flips at 100 and gives 16/16 at 100; activity first reaches eight at 500 (11/16), and gradient at 200 (11/16). Direct-write ranks neurons by their direct effect on the Q score, close to the readout that defines the flip. The Part 116 plot counts phrasings where Q is no longer first. Sources: `results/part116/README.md`, `NUMBERS.md`, and `fig-flips.png`.
- **Formatting check:** Part 115’s registered check measured the bold token’s probability, not answer correctness. An exploratory ordinary-answer check found “Paris” moved to first place after zeroing the top 100, but it was not a general-ability test. Part 115b’s plain-text instruction and final openings followed probe passes 2 and 3, so the bold-marker follow-up was post-probe. Its changed instruction, opening, and target do not isolate bold formatting. Sources: `results/part115/README.md`, `results/part115b/README.md`, and `NUMBERS.md`.
- **Relation to the four maker neurons:** Part 110 names the four as L6 N753, L7 N1169, L11 N8868, and L29 N776. L27 N3325 is a separate Google-writing neuron. The J-lens has 11 Google-family tokens for L27, 8 and 7 China-family tokens for L7/L11, and no AI-family token for L6 even though its logit lens has AI-family tokens. Part 111 does not test whether the readouts shape maker answers.
- **Prediction tally:** README prediction tables for Parts 110, 111, 114, 115, 115b, 116: 24 held and 7 failed altogether (110: 5/0; 111: 3/1; 114: 2/2; 115: 4/2; 115b: 5/2; 116: 5/0).
- **Outline adjustment:** The broader outline claim that a model's name is “held by many neurons at once” was narrowed to the observed forced answer resisting small ablations and changing under larger groups. Those experiments measure zeroing ranked MLP inputs in a forced opening, not where a model stores its name in ordinary answers. The bold-marker question is answered as unresolved, not as a formatting explanation.

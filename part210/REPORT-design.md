# Part 210 design report

Every number in this report is printed by `part210/margins210.py` from `results/part165/` (its output is
`results/part210/margins.md` and `.json`), by `part210/test_margins210.py`, or by a script named beside it.

## What shipped

- `PART210-DESIGN.md`: the design draft, in Part 202's shape, with 210.1 to 210.6 and `{{YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM}}` left in
  the registration line as the brief asks.
- `part210/margins210.py`: the published margins, answer lengths, cap counts and loop counts.
- `part210/test_margins210.py`: 16 tests, runnable with pytest or as a script; all pass.
- `results/part210/margins.md` and `margins.json`: regenerated by the script after its fix.

The first worker's `margins210.py` was read and run before anything was changed. It reproduced Part 165's published
Spearman values, all six published verdicts and the published broken counts. Three things were wrong or missing, and
are fixed:

1. `165.1`'s printed headroom read `51.00000000000002 n+ answers` (a float formatting artifact). It now prints
   `51.0`. The number itself was right.
2. `ladder_spearman` would raise `KeyError: 'full'` on any file whose ladder has no `full` row with a `k` (the
   `LADDER_K[arm] if arm != "full" else ...` expression evaluated the lookup first). It now reads `full` from the
   argument only. Part 165's own files never hit it; a fixture did.
3. The brief asks for each arm's count of answers at the 80-token cap, mean `token_count`, and primary-broken and
   loop-only counts. The script had all of these except the loop counts split by cap, and had no plain-word-count
   loop (`whitespace_loop`), which the design's loop discussion needs. A new section, "Where the broken answers sit:
   at the cap, and loops", and a `cap_and_loops` block in the JSON now print, per arm: at-cap answers, at-cap
   primary-broken, at-cap loop-only, at-cap whitespace loop, whitespace loop, primary broken.

The deliberate break (PROCEDURE.md section 5): `margins210.py` line 89's changed rule was edited in place to
`answer.split() != base.split()` (whole answer instead of the first 12 words). `test_margins210.py` then went red —
5 failed, 11 passed, among them `test_published_rates_match_score_json`, `test_published_spearman_matches_score_json`
and `test_published_165_6_interval_is_degenerate`. The file was restored from a copy taken before the edit, and the
suite went back to 16 passed. The suite also contains its own break test
(`test_the_break_turns_the_published_checks_red`), which makes a copy of the script outside the tree, breaks it, and
requires the published checks on the copy to fail; it passes, so the published checks can see a wrong changed rule.

## Part 165's design, code and results: what looks wrong or surprising

1. **165.5's bar is written two ways, and the scorer used the Predictions table.** `PART165-DESIGN.md:44` (Controls)
   says "165.5 fails if iso breaks as often as full", a bar of 1.0; `PART165-DESIGN.md:67` (Predictions) and
   `PREDICTIONS.md`'s Part 165 row say "iso's broken rate is under 0.8 times full's". `part165/score165.py:335` uses
   `ratio_less_than(..., .8)`, the 0.8 bar, and `results/part165/README.md` records the choice. The published 0.8356
   fails the 0.8 bar and would have passed the 1.0 bar. The design keeps the scorer's bar and says so.
2. **165.6's interval is degenerate because the changed rate is at the ceiling.** `part165/score165.py:207` drops a
   bootstrap draw whose Spearman is `None`. `spearman` returns `None` when either rank vector has no spread
   (`score165.py:108`), which happens when all seven ladder arm rates in a draw are equal. Six of the seven ladder arms
   have a changed rate of exactly 1.0 in every slot, and k16 has 0.9987 from one slot (layer 19 slot 2) at 0.875, so
   most draws give seven equal rates. 3,475 of 10,000 draws were dropped and the 6,525 kept all gave 0.2041, so the
   interval is 0.2041 to 0.2041. `margins210.py` reproduces 6,525 usable draws with score165's own code. The rerun
   inherits this if its changed rate stays at the ceiling, which the design says and asks its scorer to print.
3. **The primary broken rule counts 593 of 1,062 broken answers on the loop or short-answer rule alone.** Triage
   keeps an answer that names a model (`part86/triage86.py:55`), and the loop override then calls it broken anyway
   (`part165/score165.py:80-81`). 460 whitespace-trigram loops are kept by triage and broken only by the override.
   This is the registered rule, not a defect, but it means most of the "broken" count is repetition, which is why the
   cap matters (Size in the design).
4. **The loop rule's tokenizer and a plain word count disagree.** `score165.has_trigram_loop` counts triage's word
   tokens (punctuation dropped); a `str.split` count keeps punctuation attached. On the battery the two give 1,042 and
   877 answers. The design prints both.
5. **`run165.run()` cannot be wrapped into chunks of 8.** The loop at `part165/run165.py:216-218` batches at
   `args.batch_size`, `main` refuses anything but 64 (`run165.py:251`), and `run()` writes `norms.json` on every
   launch (`run165.py:180`) and records no call shapes. The design therefore rebuilds the plan, as Part 202 rebuilt
   Part 164's, and names the test that proves it equal to the 6,920 published rows.
6. **Part 165's `changed` measure is phrasing-bound.** `score165.py:154` compares against `baselines.get(qi, "")`,
   the no-push answer for that question index, so a donor answer must match the row's phrasing as well as its labels.
   The design's donor key is labels plus `question_index` and the no-push answers are held fixed in every redraw.
7. **Part 165's 165.2 is already fragile in its own data.** Without layer 21 the broken Spearman is -0.714
   (`results/part165/README.md`), below the -0.8 bar; the design's Size says so and 210.2 is low confidence.
8. **The effect run and the geometry reading do not reach 64 keys.** Tokenized in this session with the tokenizer in
   `~/.cache/huggingface/hub/models--Qwen--Qwen3.5-4B`, the 8 effect phrasings are 16 to 18 prompt tokens; the effect
   run generates 2 (`part165/effect165.py:106`), so the longest row is 20 keys and the measured step is at 19. The
   battery's longest row is 18 + 80 = 98. The reading run (`part165/reading165.py:49`, `SEQ = 128`) is forward passes
   over 128-token documents and prompt + 1 token, at most 19 keys. Neither is exposed; neither is rerun.

## Assumptions

- The local Qwen3.5-4B tokenizer in `~/.cache/huggingface/hub` (same model id as the Arc snapshot) tokenizes the same
  prompts the same way. The prompt lengths (16 to 18 tokens) are the only numbers that depend on it, and they are far
  from 64 either way.
- "Exposed" means a pushed generation whose cached key count passes 64 at some step. The effect run's prefill is
  unpushed (`AddRowsHook` leaves the prompt pass alone), and its one pushed step has 19 keys.
- The design's "6,920 rows over the six-hour task limit" pilot guard (0.32 answers/s) is a broken-path guard, as Part
  202's is; Part 165's own registered pilot rule (under 1.5 answers/s, `run165.py:232`) is not kept, because the
  8-row shape is expected to run slower than Part 165's batch of 64.
- The rerun's shape floor labels are Part 165's own rules; the `short` label is not in Part 165's scorer but is part of
  the primary rule (`score165._loop_broken`), so it is a label the donor key must carry. A drawn label combination no
  answer shows is projected with the primary rule's own precedence and counted, as Part 202 does.
- `tools/part_check.py prelaunch 210` was run. With a concrete time in the registration line it fails only on
  registration (no `## Part 210` in `PREDICTIONS.md`, not on `origin/main`, and `git fetch` cannot write its
  `FETCH_HEAD` in this sandbox); with `{{YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM}}` it also reports the placeholder, as the brief intends.

## What could not be determined

- Whether the changed rate stays at 1.0 on eager. The design predicts it does not change the verdict, but a
  non-degenerate 165.6 interval depends on it, and only the run can say.
- The wall time on the A770 for 7,120 answers in chunks of 8. The estimate (about 130 minutes) is Part 202's measured
  rate applied to this count; Part 165's own run used batch 64 and is not comparable.
- Whether a donor with the same labels and phrasing will exist for every drawn combination. Part 165's battery has
  768 rows per arm and eight phrasings, so most combinations are supported; the design counts and prints the ones that
  are not, as Part 202 does, but the count is only known after the replicate is decoded.
- Whether the kernel or the batch shape causes the cap jump. The records do not separate them, as Parts 200 and 202
  say; this design attributes changes to both together.

**[Part 206](results/part206/README.md)**, 2026-11-13. Is the thing an MLP reads the same in every conversation, who writes it, and does an assistant turn shift it? Part 206 split what MLPs and mixers write into a constant, a token part and a context part on Qwen3.5-4B (base and chat) and on OLMo 2 1B across six training stages, replicated Tuomi's 2026 gate-threshold test (GT), and asked whether an assistant frame adds one fixed vector at every passage position. The split is the same shape in both families: the first layer's MLP output is 0.556 token-determined of what varies on Qwen chat (0.270 on OLMo Instruct) and only about 0.03 constant, and by the middle layer it is 0.118 token-determined and mostly context. Tuomi's static test replicates in all eight models; his causal test in none (the duty change is 3.3 to 10.0 times the largest random direction, short of the 20 asked for). No constant assistant shift at this resolution: `rho_peak(A-tmpl, M)` is 0.106 on Qwen chat (95% interval 0.103 to 0.110) and 0.164 on OLMo Instruct, under the 0.2 bar, and taken position by position the share is only a little higher (0.117 and 0.182), so most of the frame's effect differs from position to position. The catalogued slot neurons do not arm under the assistant frame (median percentile 49.05 against the concept neurons' 46.81 at OLMo Instruct); the 16 maker and company neurons do not either (45.97). Subtracting the shift from Qwen chat raises the maker token's probability rather than lowering it (+0.0622, against +0.0857 for the null direction), and changing the role word alone carries only 0.29 of the frame's ratio. Five of the 17 registered predictions held (206.4, 206.5, 206.7, 206.9, 206.M3) and twelve failed; 206.M1 changed from held to failed in Amendment 5, a scorer fix made after the scores were seen.

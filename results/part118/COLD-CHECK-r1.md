# Part 118 cold check, round 1 (Haiku 5.5, 2026-10-30)

**Bottom line.** Every number in the README matches SCORE.md and NUMBERS.md and recomputes from the raw files. No registered verdict is more generous than its text. The problems are in what the numbers are said to mean, and in what the limits section leaves out. Two label issues could move exploratory figures. The high-dose persona counts come from a reader that over-marks relative to the second reader, in one direction only, and the README does not say so.

## Findings

**2. [changes a number, if re-labelled] An unresolved error cell reads like its persona.** p92-42, neuron, α=0.8, q0 has no label after retries (`labels.jsonl`). Its answer ("I am a native of the field of battle... a veteran of the ranks...", `answers.jsonl`) reads as a show of its persona, "Jacobite royalist veteran" (`results/part92/step2/neurons.json`). **3. [overstated] The reader disagreement runs one way and sits where the amended counts are.** In the 281-cell DS sample, Haiku marks persona on 36 cells and DS on 17. All 19 disagreements are Haiku true and DS false (`labels.jsonl` against `labels-ds.jsonl`). At α=1.6 in the neuron arm, Haiku marks 14 of 43 sampled answers and DS marks 4. My cell-level bootstrap (2,000 resamples, seed 1) puts the pooled Gate 2 kappa of 0.609 at roughly 0.45 to 0.76. Neuron-arm kappa is 0.62 and random-arm kappa is 0.33. `README.md:145` says only that the kappa "does not show that every label is reader independent." The amended 4/21 and 10/18 counts (`README.md:5`, `:34`) and the 1.6 persona counts (`SCORE.md:118-119`) are Haiku-only and likely high. Verdicts would not flip to pass, because DS would be expected to lower them. The README should say so.

**4. [overstated] The headline result is forced by the coherence collapse.** At α=1.6 every neuron answer is marked incoherent (234/234, `README.md:102`). So no unit can have a coherent persona show there, and 118.1 and 118.5 cannot pass under the registered rule whatever the sort is. The title and first paragraph (`README.md:3`), and the 118.1 and 118.5 rows (`README.md:27`, `:33`), lead with this. `PART118-DESIGN.md:125-126` says a 118.5 failure "says nothing about dose." The README says only that "no persona marked answer was coherent" (`README.md:5`).

**5. [overstated] The amended high-dose persona readings count incoherent text.** The 4/21 and 10/18 counts are built from answers the README calls incoherent at this dose. `README.md:63` quotes one, a loop of "My magic is everything!" marked as a persona show. The α=1.6 persona-only shows are 54 of 108 concept answers and 19 of 126 slot answers, none coherent (`SCORE.md:118-119`). "Persona marks" will read as persona expression. The README should say these are repetition loops that reuse the persona's wording.

**6. [overstated] The causal sentence in the reading restates the rule.** `README.md:141` says the counts vary "because different answers meet the persona and coherence rules at each dose." That is circular. The mechanism the data show is that coherence collapses at α≥1.6 in both arms (`README.md:102-103`) while persona marks persist (`SCORE.md:118-119`). The sentence should state that mechanism or drop the "because."

**7. [overstated, omission] The primary reader's own failures are not reported.** Haiku's sidecar (`results/part118/labels.usage.jsonl`) holds 2,035 attempts. 84 stopped at the 500-token cap, 43 of them at α=0.8 neuron. 79 returned HTTP 400, and all of those are on p92-00 in the first 79 rows of the run. 4 were network errors. Each failure was retried. The four unresolved cells returned the cap on every attempt that produced output, across three runs. `README.md:52` justifies the cap by Part 92's 634 of 636, not by this run's record. The limits section (`README.md:143-147`) says nothing about any of this.

**8. [overstated, omission] The 0.8 column has no margin, and it is in-sample.** Seven of 18 concept units have exactly three coherent shows at α=0.8 (p92-01, p92-11, p92-15, p92-39, p92-41, p92-43, p92-49), and five more have two. One label moves each of them across the cutoff. Gate 1b replicates a set chosen at this dose, so some drop is expected from selection alone. `README.md:87` gives 7/18 with no margin. Part 92b's README reports the same fragility for its reader (`results/part92b/README.md:5`). The note that the 0.8 column is "expected to look best" (`README.md:147`) sits in the limits, not beside the table.

**9. [minor] Gate 1b passes on a weak check.** Under the same persona-only rule, Part 92's own Haiku labels keep all 39 kinds (recomputed from `results/part92/step2/labels-instruct_chat.jsonl`). Amendment 2 switched the rule after the registered rule kept only 31 of 39 on those labels. That is disclosed (`README.md:46`) and was done before primary labels.

## Labels I read

I read about 50 distinct answers: a seeded stratified sample of 32 (four per dose and arm), the five cells the README quotes, the four unresolved error cells, and the first twelve α=0.8 persona positives. At α=1.6 and 3.2 the incoherence labels look right on every answer I read. Those answers are word salad, repetition, empty strings or loops. At α=0.4 and 0.8 most labels are defensible. The ones I would question are: p92-11 q3 (finding 1); the α=1.6 loops marked as persona (finding 5); the α=0.4 p92-49 positive (item 10); and the unresolved p92-42 q0 and p92-33 q1 (finding 2). The p92-00 q3 positive, which describes flowing water with rocks and boulders, is defensible under the README's "topic or imagery" rubric (`README.md:157`).

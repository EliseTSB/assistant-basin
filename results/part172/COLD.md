# COLD check of results/part172/README.md

**Verdict.** The numbers hold and the registered verdicts follow the rules. I recomputed every arm's cell counts from `answers.jsonl` (54 of 54 arms match `score.json`), reproduced the bootstrap intervals for 172.1, 172.2 and 172.9 and both frame contrasts at seed 172, checked the d = 1 control table and the dose ladder row by row, confirmed the exploratory maker counts, and found all 20 quoted answers verbatim. The claim-versus-mention split is correctly unread. The problem is framing. The README treats n1's d = 1 lead over the controls as its specificity result and calls the caveat "dose coverage", but its own ladder shows that at matched damage one control set (r100 draw 2171) and one single neuron (n2) give more clean Google-family answers with zero broken answers than n1 does at any dose. A writer sentence says "not a route through L27 N3325", which the d = 0.75 contrast contradicts. Several things a skeptic needs are missing: the writer bands, empty hand-read strata, the definition of the control families, an answer to the single-neuron question, a method summary and a source for the run-history claim. The README should not be read as a specificity result until findings 1 and 2 are fixed. Numbers: sound. Framing: overstated.

## Findings

**1. [high] Lead paragraph and the 172.2 row overstate n1's specificity; the d = 1 comparison is not dose-matched.**
README line 3: "The main qualification is dose coverage: 10 of the 12 control sets were measured only at d = 1, while r100 draw 2171 had 44 of 44 clean family hits at d = 0.75 and 1 at d = 1."
README line 31: "The held 172.2 verdict is a comparison at d = 1; it does not establish that n1 exceeds every control at each control's best dose."
What is wrong: dose coverage is not the whole qualification. From the README's own ladder (lines 60 to 78) and my recount: r100 draw 2171 at d = 0.5 gives 35 clean family hits and 0 broken answers, against n1's 4 hits and 0 broken. At d = 0.75 it gives 44 hits and 0 broken, against n1's 18 hits and 4 broken. At d = 1 that control has 1 clean hit and 30 broken answers, and the clean-only count reads the breakage as absence of names. So the 172.2 "held" result comes from comparing n1 at d = 1 with a control that is overdosed there. The 172.2 row (line 12) has no dose qualifier at all. The verdict is correct under the rule, but the sentence in line 31 is weaker than the data.
Fix: replace the line 3 sentence "The main qualification is dose coverage: ..." with: "The d = 1 comparison is not dose-matched. At d = 0.5 and 0.75, r100 draw 2171 gives 35 and 44 clean family hits with no broken answers, against n1's 4 (0 broken) and 18 (4 broken). The specificity result therefore holds at d = 1 only, where that control is overdosed." Replace line 31's final sentence with: "At matched damage, one control exceeds n1 on clean hits, so 172.2 does not show that n1 names Google more than the controls at any dose other than d = 1." Add to the 172.2 observed-number cell: "held at d = 1 only; at d = 0.5 r100 draw 2171 has 35 clean hits and 0 broken, n1 has 4."

**2. [high] "not a route through L27 N3325" contradicts the d = 0.75 writer contrast; the band labels the design requires are missing.**
README line 110: "This is dependence under this intervention, not a route through L27 N3325."
What is wrong: the README's own writer table (lines 96 to 98) shows the writer matters at d = 0.5 and 0.75. Zeroing L27 N3325 takes clean family hits from 4 to 1 at d = 0.5 and from 18 to 6 at d = 0.75, losing 13 of 18 paired hits at 0.75. Only at d = 1 is there no net loss (172.6 failed). "Not a route" is therefore wrong at 0.75, and it goes beyond 172.6, which tests d = 1 alone. The design reads this contrast at 0.5, 0.75 and 1.0 and makes no claim about routes. The word "dependence" is used for a null result at d = 1, which reads badly. The design's band rule (for n1, off/on at or below one third is low, at or above two thirds is high, between is partial) is also never applied: at 0.5 the ratio is 25.0% (low), at 0.75 it is 33.3% (low, on the boundary), and at 1.0 it is 105.9% (high by net count) while paired retention is 76.5%.
Fix: replace the sentence with: "At d = 1 the net count does not fall with the writer off (172.6 failed; 13 of 17 paired hits survive). At d = 0.75 the writer-off run keeps 5 of 18 paired hits. The writer contrast therefore depends on dose. This does not show a route through L27 N3325, and it does not rule one out." Under the writer table, add: "Bands by the design rule (net off/on): 0.5 low (25.0%); 0.75 low (33.3%, on the boundary); 1.0 high by net count (105.9%), with paired retention 76.5%."

**3. [medium] The single-neuron question is answered only at d = 1, and the d = 0.5 singles are reported partly.**
README line 116: "The lower dose comparison shows that n2's d = 1 row is not a dose-independent estimate." and "Prediction 172.8 fails because the registered bar required at least two singles."
What is wrong: design question 4 asks whether L20 N492 is alone, or whether the next-ranked singles do the same. The README answers only through 172.8 at d = 1 and never states an answer. My recount at d = 0.5: n2 gives 44 of 44 clean hits and 0 broken (30 with Qwen retained); n5 gives 12 clean hits and 0 broken, which is not mentioned anywhere; n4 gives 4 hits and 0 broken, also not mentioned; n3 gives 0 hits, 15 Qwen-only, 29 neither and 0 broken. So at matched damage n2 beats n1 at every dose, and n5 also produces damage-free Google-family names. The reader cannot see this without recomputing.
Fix: add a table of the four singles at d = 0.5 and d = 1.0 (clean hits, broken, GS with its denominator, Qwen retained) beside the n1 ladder, and add one sentence: "At d = 1 only n4 reaches the GS bar. At d = 0.5, n2 (44 hits, 0 broken) and n5 (12 hits, 0 broken) also give clean Google-family names, so L20 N492 is not the only single that does. The 172.8 failure is a d = 1 result and does not answer question 4."

**4. [medium] The control families are never defined, and "matched" means only written length.**
README lines 27 to 50 and 52 to 54 use "r1", "r10", "r100" and "rand100" by draw number and never say what they are.
What is wrong: from `part171/run171.py` (lines 495 to 510 and about 540 to 584) and `results/part172/sets-check.txt`: n1 is the single top coordinate by |delta × p|, L20 N492. rand100 is 100 random coordinates in total, placed with the per-layer counts of n100 (kind "sparse"). r1, r10 and r100 are random orthonormal directions, dense across each layer's 9,216-wide space (kind "basis"), with per-layer counts copied from the n1, n10 and n100 sets. The design's "matched" means equal written length (4|v| at d = 1) only; a single neuron, sparse coordinates and dense directions are different kinds of write. The families differ in structure, and that matters for the d = 1 comparison: the sparse rand100 set rises and then breaks between d = 0.75 and d = 1 (4 hits and 0 broken, then 14 and 13), while the dense r100 set gives its name shift at lower doses. Neither a reader nor a reviewer can judge the controls from the README.
Fix: add under "The d = 1 comparison and its limit": "Control families. n1 is the single top coordinate, L20 N492. rand100 is 100 random coordinates in total, placed with the per-layer counts of n100. r1, r10 and r100 are random orthonormal directions, dense across each layer's space, with per-layer counts copied from the n1, n10 and n100 sets. All 12 are matched to n1 for written length (4|v| at d = 1), not for structure. See part171/run171.py, lines 495 to 510 and about 540 to 584."

**5. [low] The hit rule silently drops sentences with "not", so one Google answer is scored neither; the rule is not stated.**
README line 214 (exploratory, rand100 draw 171): "I am an AI model developed by Google, and I do not have a parent company."
What is wrong: `run171.hit_match`, which the scorer uses, skips any sentence containing not, never, n't or 不是 before the alias test. This answer names Google but is scored neither, and the README lists it as a "maker" under clean-neither without comment. Six clean answers are affected: rand100_d171 new44:35 (neither), rand100_d2171 (neither), n4_d1 (neither), n4_d0.5, ko0.5 and ko0.75 (each Qwen). No n1 arm is affected, and no verdict changes; rand100_d171's GS would move from 14/31 to 15/32, still well above 172.3's bar.
Fix: add to the method summary: "A sentence with not, never, n't or 不是 is not counted as a hit, so 'developed by Google, and I do not ...' is scored as no hit; six clean answers are affected, none in an n1 arm." In the exploratory item, add: "scored neither by the hit rule; the answer names Google."

**6. [medium] The frame section does not apply the design's conditional reading and does not state the 2 × 2 result.**

What is wrong: the design says that if 172.9 to 172.12 hold, a shared frame is consistent with a frame-mediated account, and that this does not show it. 172.10 failed (the forced opening alone gives Google on 4 of 44), so the condition is not met, and the README never says so. A reader who sees 172.9, 172.11 and 172.12 held will read them as support for a frame account. The 2 × 2, which the design calls "the test", is given only as bare numbers: the push adds 19 clean hits inside each fixed opening (frame_n1 4 to n1_frame_n1 23; frame_plain 0 to n1_frame_plain 19). I reproduced both paired intervals (11 to 27 and 13 to 25).
Fix: after line 124 add: "172.10 failed: the forced 'I am a model trained by' opening alone gives Google on 4 of 44. So the frame-consistency condition in the design's reading rule is not met. The 2 × 2 shows the push adds 19 clean family hits inside each fixed opening: 4 to 23 under the n1 opening, and 0 to 19 under the plain one."

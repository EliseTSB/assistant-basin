# Cold check of results/part176/README.md (Part 176)

Checked against: PART176-DESIGN.md (whole, incl. Reading rules, Limits, Amendment 1), results/part176/ files (answers.jsonl, labels-ds.jsonl, labels-haiku.jsonl, score.json, prediction_table.json, scored_directions.jsonl, transitions.json, numbers176.py/json, labels/audit.json and audit.jsonl, gates.json, readers.log, labels/retry_net.jsonl, plot176.png), and the stored Part 173 to 175 files. Recomputation used the repo's own part173 scorer for the broken/clean flags and the reader join, with `-I -B`; state, support, L, gap, transition and sensitivity counts were recomputed independently from answers and labels. No writes to the repo, the Arc, or any paid API.

## Verdict

The README's core numbers hold. I rebuilt numbers176.py and README.md from the frozen files in a scratch copy and got byte-identical output, so the prose is generated from score.json and nothing is hand-edited into the numbers. My recomputed answer states at all five rungs match the README table exactly; support (20, 30, 20, 10, 0), Google-led (30 of 30), the 18/12 sign split, median L (0.268), the 176.7 rho (+0.016 on 37 eligible), the W3 median gap (0.0177, 22 under 0.025), the transition sums and the family counts (3, 28, 29 of 36, 120, 120, 200 rows; 0 other-led) all reproduce. The pre-registered verdicts follow the registered rules. The problems are in what the prose claims beyond those numbers: the title's "close to where its answers fall apart" is not a measured share; one trajectory percentage is computed on the wrong denominator; the 0.225 inert-dose guard is reported as unreadable for a reason that is wrong (the guard is fully readable and gives 880 of 880 per sign differing from none); the 176.W3 verdict sits on the 0.025 falsifier line under two of the five sensitivity rules, which the README does not say; and the 0.225 window-versus-peak question is answered by an unstated shape. Several reporting requirements in the design are missing (direction-level table, 176.7 exclusions and conditional measures, Wilson intervals, the 0.225 guard, two checks, several Limits).

## Findings (most serious first)

1. **High, wrong denominator.** README, "Across complete trajectories, 1031 of 1760 ever reached Google. 893 of those ended failed (58.4%)." Recomputed: 893 of 1031 ever-Google trajectories ended failed at 0.30, which is 86.6%. The 58.4% is 893 of the 1529 trajectories that ended failed, i.e. the share of failed endings that passed through Google (transitions.json `failed_end_after_google_share`). Fix: "893 of those 1031 (86.6%) ended failed; 893 of the 1529 trajectories that ended failed (58.4%) had passed through Google." Also regenerate by fixing the template in numbers176.py around line 774.

2. **High, wrong reason and a readable guard reported as unread.** README, "its 0.225 check is unread because of incomplete or duplicate rows." Rows are complete. `_inert_guard` in score176.py selects rows with `set` starting `f100d_d`, which also matches the two replay arms (`f100d_d174104`, `f100d_d174115`, 88 rows), so the count is 1848 not 1760. Recomputed on the new draws only: at +0.225, 880 of 880 differ from `none` in the first 40 tokens; at −0.225, 880 of 880; share 1.0, so not inert. The design requires the share at every rung. Fix: exclude replay sets from the guard filter (match the new-draw set names), rerun score176 and numbers176, and state "0.225: 1760 of 1760 differ from none (share 1.00); the earlier 'not read' came from replay rows in the filter."

3. **High, 176.W3 sits on the falsifier line under alternative rules, and the README does not say so.** README reports L medians under the five sensitivity rules but not W3. Recomputed median gap under those rules: registered 0.0177 (22 of 34 under 0.025); failed threshold 15: 0.0112; failed threshold 29: 0.0250 (stored as 0.024999…; on the line, not below in any meaningful sense); fragment cut 8: 0.0250 (same); fragment cut 20: 0.0169; lost 22: 0.0144. Under the registered wording, a median exactly on 0.025 is neither the claim (≥0.05) nor the falsifier (<0.025), so the design calls it inconclusive. Fix: add a W3 row per sensitivity rule to the L sensitivity table, and state: "The W3 failure holds at the registered proxy and at threshold 15, lost and fragment 20; at threshold 29 and fragment 8 the median gap sits on 0.025 and the falsifier is not met in any meaningful sense." Change the W3 verdict sentence to say its robustness.

4. **High, title and first paragraph claim more than measured.** Title: "...and the top of each direction's band sits close to where its answers fall apart." Measured: among the 34 directions with a supported rung, 22 have gap under 0.025 (65%); that is 22 of 40 (55%) of all directions; 6 directions have no supported rung and so no band; 12 have gap ≥0.025 (up to 0.066); 1 gap is negative (−0.0125) and 1 is zero. "Close" is not defined in the README except through the 0.025 falsifier, which is the W3 result that failed. Fix: retitle to say the measured thing, e.g. "...; for 22 of 34 directions with a supported rung, the top supported rung is within one rung (0.025) of their interpolated collapse dose." Remove "each direction's band" and "close."

5. **Medium, the W3 gap explanation is asserted, not computed, and the falsifier is the grid.** README: "This part of the small gap can come from a steep change plus rung spacing alone." With 0.025 spacing, gap < 0.025 is nearly the same event as "top supported rung = lower end of L's bracket": 19 directions by the stored field (20 by my boundary convention), which accounts for most of the 22. The README does not state that identity, does not account for the other three under-0.025 gaps, and computes no null. Fix: state the identity; say that under a sharp step with L uniform in its bracket the gap is uniform on [0, 0.025) (median ≈0.0125, all under 0.025), so the observed 22 of 34 is only partly what a step alone gives; the 12 directions with gap ≥0.025 are support ending a rung before the proxy crossing, which a step alone does not produce.

6. **Medium, negative and zero gaps omitted.** Design: "a negative gap is reported as it is." README reports only the 22 under 0.025 and the median. Two directions have top supported rung at or above L: 176107+ (0.275 supported, L 0.2625, gap −0.0125) and 176113+ (L 0.275, gap 0.0). Fix: list both in the W3 paragraph; say support persisted past the failure proxy in one direction.

7. **Medium, window/plateau/slope question not answered in words.** Section "Is 0.225 a window, plateau, or slope?" says "not a 1-rung window" and "W1 slope form failed." The data show 0.225 is the peak of the ladder: 30 versus 20–21 at 0.20 and 20 at 0.25, exceeding both neighbours by 9–10 directions, which is the W1 falsifier. Fix: state "0.225 is a peak on this five-rung ladder: 30 supported against 20–21 at 0.20 and 20 at 0.25; the W1 falsifier (a peak of ≥5 over both neighbours) fires." The "flat band" wording in the title should be reconciled with that.

8. **Medium, required direction-level table missing.** Design: "Every signed direction is reported alone, with its five rungs," and the direction table "shows each direction's Google peak, its top supported rung and L, with the warning that a peak cannot sit far above L." README only lists scored_directions.jsonl as "all signed directions and 5 rung rows." Fix: add the table (40 rows; Google peak, top supported rung, L, gap, the warning) or state its absence as a deviation.

9. **Medium, 176.7 reporting incomplete.** README: "on 37 eligible directions from 19 draws." The 3 excluded directions (reason "Google count null at 0.225": 176110+, 176110−, 176120−) are not named. The L split-half (0.92) is reported instead of the share measure. Fix: add the exclusion reasons, the share split-half, the 0.20/0.25 Spearmans, and the conditional measures. The 176.7 non-detection itself is robust: recomputed rho over the six rules is +0.016, +0.043, −0.066, +0.087, +0.031, +0.051, all above −0.30.

10. **Medium, audit "12 were empty" is wrong.** README, "The second reader returned 163 of 175 audit replies; 12 were empty." audit.jsonl: 10 "empty response", 1 DNS name-resolution error (`URLError gaierror`), 1 "no JSON object" reply; each after 2 attempts. Also note the audit reader's failures are outside the 0.5% cap as written.
